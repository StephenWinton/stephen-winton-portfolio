Selected Case Studies

Transformation leadership in practice

AI ENABLEMENT

Turning AI Experimentation into an Adopted Operating Capability

Program and delivery teams were spending significant time on repetitive administrative and reporting work. I conceived, designed, built, and scaled an internal AI-enabled operating capability for PMO and delivery workflows.

  • Reusable AI-assisted workflows rather than one-off prompting
  • Planning, reporting, documentation, backlog, capacity, and dashboard support
  • Onboarding, training, office hours, documentation, and support
  • Expanded for use across a 15-person PMO organization
Read the leadership lesson

Successful enterprise AI adoption is not primarily about access to a model. It depends on understanding the work, standardizing the workflow, creating trust, supporting users, and connecting technology to measurable operating needs.

DELIVERY TRANSFORMATION

Improving Planning, Delivery Discipline, and Operating Visibility

Multiple technology teams operated with different working patterns, planning practices, delivery cadences, and maturity levels. I led transformation efforts that created clearer operating structures while preserving flexibility for different technical functions.

  • Cross-team quarterly planning and dependency coordination
  • Delivery-health dashboards and leadership metrics
  • Jira hierarchy, workflow conventions, and structured escalation
  • Scrumban-based model introduced in DevOps and DBA environments
Read the leadership lesson

Transformation works best when structure is introduced to solve a business problem—not simply to enforce a methodology. The goal is clearer priorities, faster decisions, more predictable delivery, and understandable accountability.

Transformation Leadership Framework

From operating friction to sustainable capability

A repeatable approach used across program leadership, delivery transformation, PMO modernization, process improvement, and AI enablement.

1

Diagnose Friction

Understand the work as it actually happens before selecting a methodology or tool.

2

Define Outcomes

Clarify what should become easier, faster, clearer, or more predictable.

3

Create Visibility

Use dashboards, capacity views, risk and exception reporting, and clear sources of truth.

4

Design the Operating Model

Define planning cadence, ownership, workflows, escalation, decision points, and reporting rhythms.

5

Enable & Automate

Coach people, document the model, and automate repetitive high-friction work where it creates value.

6

Measure & Adjust

Evaluate flow, predictability, adoption, workload alignment, exceptions, and remaining ambiguity.

7

Scale What Works

Turn local success into reusable templates, dashboards, training, workflows, and shared practices.

“A transformation becomes durable when the improved way of working no longer depends on one person remembering how to do it.”
