# Case Study: Enterprise AI Enablement

## Turning AI Experimentation into an Adopted Operating Capability

### Challenge

Program and delivery teams were spending significant time on repetitive administrative and reporting work. Planning inputs, ticket quality, delivery reporting, and workflow standards varied across teams, while PMO reporting required substantial manual effort.

The opportunity was not simply to introduce an AI tool. It was to create a practical operating capability that could be adopted by program managers and delivery teams without disrupting the systems they already used.

### Leadership Approach

I conceived, designed, built, and scaled an internal AI-enabled operating capability for PMO and delivery workflows. I served across the full lifecycle—from identifying high-friction work and designing the solution through rollout, enablement, support, and continuous improvement.

The operating model emphasized:

- Reusable AI-assisted workflows rather than one-off prompting
- Integration with existing work-management and knowledge tools
- Consistent planning, reporting, and documentation practices
- Human review and approval for consequential actions
- Team-specific configuration without creating fragmented solutions
- Clear onboarding, guidance, and support for non-technical users

### What I Built and Led

The capability evolved into a broad workflow platform supporting activities such as:

- Work intake and structured ticket creation
- Description and documentation quality
- Backlog health and refinement support
- Sprint and delivery operations
- Program and portfolio reporting
- Quarterly planning and exception analysis
- Capacity and workload visibility
- Executive delivery-health dashboards
- Team onboarding and configuration
- Reusable prompt, workflow, and automation libraries

I also led the adoption model around the platform, including onboarding, training, office hours, documentation, support channels, and ongoing feature improvement based on user needs.

### Scale

The solution expanded from an individual innovation into an adopted PMO capability supporting a **15-person PMO team** and multiple product-engineering and shared-services use cases.

The underlying capability library grew to include dozens of reusable workflows, skills, prompts, and team-specific configurations, creating a repeatable framework rather than a collection of isolated automations.

### Business Value

The program helped:

- Reduce repetitive manual administration
- Improve consistency in planning and reporting outputs
- Strengthen ticket and backlog quality
- Increase access to structured delivery information
- Improve executive and PMO visibility
- Accelerate recurring analysis and reporting tasks
- Create a scalable model for AI-assisted program management
- Move AI usage from individual experimentation toward repeatable team adoption

### What This Demonstrates

This work demonstrates the ability to combine:

**Transformation leadership** — identifying operating friction and converting it into a scalable improvement agenda.

**Product and platform thinking** — designing reusable capabilities that support multiple teams and workflows.

**Change adoption** — driving onboarding, training, support, and practical use by both technical and non-technical users.

**Operating-model design** — embedding new capabilities into existing planning, reporting, and delivery processes.

**AI enablement** — applying AI to real enterprise work while maintaining appropriate human control and process discipline.

### Transferable Leadership Lesson

The most important lesson was that successful enterprise AI adoption is not primarily about access to a model. It depends on understanding the work, standardizing the workflow, creating trust, supporting users, and connecting the technology to measurable operating needs.

That same approach is transferable to broader enterprise transformation, PMO modernization, operational excellence, and technology-change initiatives.
